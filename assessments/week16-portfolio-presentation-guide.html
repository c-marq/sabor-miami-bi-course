Portfolio Presentation Guide

Week 16 · Monday · Group Presentation · 150 Points

💡 WHAT YOU ARE PRESENTING You and your partner are presenting the Sabor Miami Power BI portfolio that you have been building all semester. In 5 minutes, you will walk the class through what you discovered in the data, the challenges you faced, how you overcame those challenges, and what you would do next with more time. You are presenting from a single shared .pbix file.

Step 1 — Wednesday Tutoring Clinic

✅ DO THIS — during the Wednesday tutoring block
  1. Both partners open their Lab 4 files side by side. Compare: whose file has the cleaner model? The better dashboard layout? The more complete set of measures?
  2. Pick one file as the base. Save it as Team_LastName1_LastName2_Portfolio.pbix. This is your shared portfolio file from now on.
  3. Cherry-pick from the other file. If your partner has a visual you like, recreate it in the shared file. If they have a measure you do not have, add it. The goal is one file that represents the best of both partners' work.
  4. Polish the dashboard page. Add a title text box. Align your visuals. Make sure the slicer works. Think about what story this page tells about Sabor Miami's food truck business.
  5. Plan who says what. Use the presentation structure below to assign each segment to a partner. Fill in the planning worksheet.

Step 2 — The 5-Minute Presentation

🎤 Presentation Format

Both partners present from the same Power BI file, projected on screen. Each partner speaks. You do not need slides — the Power BI report IS your visual. Navigate through it as you talk.

Total time: 5 minutes. Your instructor will give a 1-minute warning.

Segment 1 · The Data Story ~1.5 min

Who speaks:

Tell the class about the Sabor Miami data. Cover:

  • What is Sabor Miami? (food truck company, 4 trucks, Miami area)
  • What did the raw data look like? What was messy about it?
  • Pick 1–2 specific data quality issues you fixed and explain what you did and why.
  • How did you combine the tables? What was the fact table vs. the dimensions?

Who speaks:

  • Switch to the dashboard page. Walk through 2–3 visuals and explain what they reveal about the business.

Segment 3 · Challenges and Next Steps ~2 min

Who speaks: Both partners

This is the most important part. Be honest. Cover:

  • What was the hardest part of this project? Pick one specific challenge — not "everything was hard." Name the task, the tool, and the moment it felt stuck.
  • How did you overcome it? Did you re-read the textbook? Ask a classmate? Undo and try again? Trace the error message? Name the specific action that got you unstuck.
  • What would you do next with more time? More measures? Better visuals? A second dashboard page for a different audience? Time intelligence? RLS for different truck managers? This shows you are thinking beyond the assignment.

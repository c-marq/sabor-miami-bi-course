Building and Connecting Your Date Table

🚀 LAUNCH PAD

What you are building: A complete Date table — a dedicated calendar dimension connected to your Sabor Miami star schema

Tool: Power BI Desktop → Table View (build), Model View (connect), Report View (verify)

File to open: Your Sabor Miami .pbix file from Session 12A — the file with all 5 tables and 3 relationships configured

Time estimate: 25–30 minutes

Steps: 15 steps across 4 phases

What "done" looks like: A six-table star schema with a Date table containing Year, Month Number, Month Name, Quarter, Day of Week, Is Weekend, and Day Number columns — verified with a Matrix visual showing sales by quarter and month in the correct order

Start here → Open your Sabor Miami .pbix file and switch to Table View (second icon on the left sidebar — looks like a grid)

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS?

Your sales table already has a date column. You can drag it into a visual and see sales by date. So why build a whole separate table?

Three reasons:

  1. Time intelligence requires it. In Chapter 12, you will write DAX measures like "year-to-date sales" and "same period last year." Those functions require a marked Date table to work. Without one, they produce errors.
  2. Grouping by month, quarter, or year requires date attributes. The sales[date] column contains full dates (2024-01-15). If you want to group by "January" or "Q1," you need separate columns for Month Name and Quarter. Those live in the Date table.
  3. Continuous date coverage. What if no sales happened on a holiday? Your sales table has no row for that date. A Date table has every date — including days with zero sales — which makes charts continuous and time comparisons accurate.

Almost every Power BI model in the professional world has a Date table. Building one is a standard skill you will use on every project.

Phase 1 of 4: Setup

Step 1: Verify your starting point

  1. Open your Sabor Miami .pbix file in Power BI Desktop
  2. Make sure you are in the main Power BI Desktop window — the ribbon should be dark blue or gray (not green). If you see a green ribbon, click Close & Apply first
  3. Switch to Table View — click the second icon on the left sidebar (looks like a grid)
🛑 STOP AND CHECK

In the Data pane (right side of the screen), you should see your 5 tables: sales, menu_items, trucks, employees, and events. If any are missing, go back to Model View and check your file.

Step 2: Examine the dates in your Sales table

  1. In the Data pane, click on the sales table
  2. Find the date column and scroll through it
  3. Notice the date format and the range — what is the earliest date? What is the latest?

Phase 2 of 4: Build the Date Table

Step 3: Create a new calculated table

  1. Find It: Click the Modeling tab in the ribbon at the top of the screen
  2. Find It: In the Calculations section (left side of the Modeling ribbon), find the New Table button
  3. Do It: Click New Table
  4. A formula bar appears at the top of the screen with the cursor blinking

WHERE AM I? You should be in Table View with the Modeling tab active in the ribbon. The formula bar should be visible at the top of the work area, ready for you to type.

Step 4: Type the CALENDARAUTO formula

In the formula bar, type the following exactly as shown, then press Enter:

Date = CALENDARAUTO()

What this does: CALENDARAUTO() scans every date column across all tables in your model, finds the earliest and latest dates, and generates a complete calendar covering that full range. For Sabor Miami, that means every day in 2024 (and possibly some days before and after, depending on your data).

🛑 STOP AND CHECK

After pressing Enter, a new table called Date should appear in the Data pane on the right. It has a single column also called Date, containing a list of dates — one row per day.

Check the status bar at the bottom of the screen — it shows the row count. For Sabor Miami, expect approximately 365–730 rows (one or two years of dates).

If you see an error: Make sure you typed the formula exactly: Date = CALENDARAUTO() — including the equals sign at the beginning and the parentheses at the end.

Step 5: Add a Year column

Now your Date table has dates, but no way to group by year. Let us add that.

  1. Make sure the Date table is selected in the Data pane
  2. Click the Modeling tab in the ribbon
  3. Click New Column (in the Calculations section, right next to New Table)
  4. In the formula bar, type the following and press Enter:
Year = YEAR('Date'[Date])

What this does: The YEAR() function extracts the four-digit year from each date. A new column called Year appears in the Date table with values like 2024.

Step 6: Add Month Number, Month Name, and Quarter columns

Repeat the New Column process three more times. For each formula below, click New Column on the Modeling tab, type the formula, and press Enter.

Month Number:

Month Number = MONTH('Date'[Date])

Month Name:

Month Name = FORMAT('Date'[Date], "MMMM")

Quarter:

Quarter = "Q" & QUARTER('Date'[Date])

What these do:

  • MONTH() returns the month as a number (1–12)
  • FORMAT() with "MMMM" (four capital M's) returns the full month name ("January," "February," etc.)
  • QUARTER() returns the quarter number (1–4), and the "Q" & adds the letter Q in front ("Q1," "Q2," etc.)
🛑 STOP AND CHECK

Your Date table should now have 5 columns: Date, Year, Month Number, Month Name, and Quarter. Scroll through the data to verify:

  • Year shows 2024 (and possibly 2023 or 2025 depending on the range)
  • Month Number shows values 1 through 12
  • Month Name shows full names like "January," "February"
  • Quarter shows "Q1" through "Q4"

If Month Name shows numbers instead of names: Check that you typed "MMMM" (four capital M's) in the FORMAT function, not "MM" (which gives two-digit month numbers).

Step 7: Add Day of Week and Is Weekend columns

Click New Column two more times for these formulas:

Day of Week:

Day of Week = FORMAT('Date'[Date], "dddd")

Is Weekend:

Is Weekend = IF(WEEKDAY('Date'[Date], 2) > 5, "Weekend", "Weekday")

What these do:

  • FORMAT() with "dddd" returns the full day name ("Monday," "Tuesday," etc.)
  • WEEKDAY() with the second parameter 2 uses Monday = 1 through Sunday = 7. If the result is greater than 5 (Saturday = 6 or Sunday = 7), the formula returns "Weekend"; otherwise it returns "Weekday"

Step 8: Add a Day Number column (for sorting)

Click New Column one more time:

Day Number = WEEKDAY('Date'[Date], 2)

What this does: Returns a number from 1 (Monday) to 7 (Sunday). You will use this column to sort Day of Week correctly — so Monday comes before Tuesday, not after Thursday (which is what happens with alphabetical sorting).

🛑 STOP AND CHECK

Your Date table should now have 8 columns: Date, Year, Month Number, Month Name, Quarter, Day of Week, Is Weekend, and Day Number. Scroll through to verify:

  • January rows should show Month Number = 1, Month Name = "January," Quarter = "Q1"
  • A Monday row should show Day of Week = "Monday," Day Number = 1, Is Weekend = "Weekday"
  • A Saturday row should show Day Number = 6, Is Weekend = "Weekend"

Phase 3 of 4: Configure

Step 9: Sort Month Name by Month Number

Without this step, Power BI will sort your months alphabetically — April before January, December before February. That is wrong. Here is how to fix it:

  1. Click the Month Name column header in the Date table (make sure the column is selected — it should be highlighted)
  2. Look at the ribbon — a Column tools tab should appear (this tab only shows when a column is selected)
  3. Click the Column tools tab
  4. Click Sort by Column
  5. Select Month Number from the dropdown

Now "January" sorts as 1, "February" as 2, and so on — chronological order instead of alphabetical.

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKE

Forgetting the Sort by Column step is one of the most common issues in Power BI reports. If your charts ever show months in the wrong order (April, August, December, February...), this is the fix. Professionals do this on every Date table they build.

Step 10: Sort Day of Week by Day Number

Same process for days of the week:

  1. Click the Day of Week column header
  2. Click the Column tools tab in the ribbon
  3. Click Sort by Column
  4. Select Day Number

Now days sort Monday through Sunday instead of alphabetically (which would put Friday before Monday and Wednesday last).

After sorting, hide the Day Number column — it was only needed for sorting:

  1. Right-click the Day Number column header
  2. Select Hide in report view

The column still exists in the model (and still controls sorting), but it will not clutter the Fields pane when building reports.

Step 11: Mark as Date Table

This step registers your Date table as the official calendar for the entire model. Without it, time intelligence DAX functions in Chapter 12 will not work.

  1. Make sure the Date table is selected in the Data pane
  2. Look at the ribbon — click the Table tools tab (this tab appears when a table is selected)
  3. Click Mark as Date Table
  4. A dialog appears asking which column contains the dates — select the Date column
  5. Click OK
🛑 STOP AND CHECK

After marking the table, the Date table icon in the Data pane should change — it now shows a small calendar symbol next to the table name. This confirms Power BI recognizes it as the official calendar.

If you see a validation error: It usually means the Date column has gaps (missing dates) or duplicate dates. CALENDARAUTO() should not produce either. Check that your formula reads exactly Date = CALENDARAUTO() and that the Date column contains continuous dates with no blanks.

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS?

Marking a table as a Date table does two things:

  1. It disables Power BI's automatic date hierarchy (which can be confusing and inconsistent)
  2. It enables time intelligence functions — DAX functions like TOTALYTD, SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR, and DATEADD that you will use in Chapter 12

Think of it as registering your calendar with Power BI: "This is THE calendar. Use this one for all time-based calculations."

Step 12: Create the relationship to Sales

  1. Switch to Model View — click the third icon on the left sidebar (three connected boxes)
  2. Find the new Date table box on the canvas. It may appear off to the side or overlapping other tables — drag it to a clear spot near the sales table
  3. Drag the Date column from the Date table onto the date column in the sales table
  4. A relationship line appears
  5. Double-click the line to verify:
    • Cardinality: One to many (1:*)
    • Cross filter direction: Single
  6. Click OK

Now rearrange your tables so the star shape is clear: sales in the center, and all dimension tables — including the new Date table — radiating outward.

🛑 STOP AND CHECK

Your Model View should now show 6 tables — the original 5 from Session 12A, plus the new Date table. You should see 4 relationship lines radiating from sales:

  • sales → trucks
  • sales → menu_items
  • sales → employees
  • sales → Date

The events table is present but unconnected (correct for now). The overall shape is a star — one fact table in the center, five dimension tables around it.

Phase 4 of 4: Verify

Step 13: Create a Matrix visual to test quarterly and monthly grouping

  1. Switch to Report View — click the first icon on the left sidebar (bar chart icon)
  2. In the Visualizations pane (right side), click the Matrix visual icon — it looks like a grid with rows and columns (different from the Table icon)
  3. An empty Matrix visual appears on the canvas
  4. In the Data pane (far right), expand the Date table
  5. Drag Quarter to the Rows well of the visual
  6. Drag Month Name to the Rows well, below Quarter — this creates a drill-down grouping (quarters containing months)
  7. From the sales table, drag amount to the Values well

You should see quarterly subtotals (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4) that you can expand to see monthly breakdowns.

To expand the quarters: Click the + icon next to a quarter label, or use the expand/collapse icons in the visual header (the small arrows at the top of the visual).

🛑 STOP AND CHECK

Your Matrix visual should show:

  • Quarterly rows (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4) with sales totals
  • When expanded, monthly rows underneath each quarter in chronological order (January, February, March under Q1 — not alphabetical)
  • Different amounts for each month/quarter

✅ Months in chronological order with different amounts = Your Date table, relationship, and Sort by Column are all working correctly. 🎉

❌ Months in alphabetical order (April, August, December...) = Go back and complete Step 9 — sort Month Name by Month Number.

❌ All months show the same amount = The relationship between Date and Sales is missing or misconfigured. Go to Model View and verify the connection.

Step 14: Create a Table visual to test Day of Week

  1. Click an empty area on the canvas to deselect the Matrix visual
  2. Click the Table visual icon in the Visualizations pane
  3. From the Date table, check Day of Week
  4. From the sales table, check amount
🛑 STOP AND CHECK

Your Table visual should show 7 rows (Monday through Sunday) with different sales amounts. The days should be in Monday-through-Sunday order — not alphabetical.

✅ Days in correct order = Your Sort by Column step worked.

❌ Days in alphabetical order (Friday, Monday, Saturday...) = Go back and complete Step 10 — sort Day of Week by Day Number.

Press Ctrl + S to save your work.

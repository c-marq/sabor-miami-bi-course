The Challenge

You already have 2 visuals from Lab #2 (a Card and a Bar Chart). Your challenge is to build 3 more visuals, add a slicer that filters everything, and give your dashboard a title. Every visual uses a column that only exists because of your merges — this is your data pipeline in action.

💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? In the portfolio project (Week 16), you will build a full dashboard page with at least 4 visuals. This is a low-pressure preview of that work. Nothing you do here is graded — experiment, try things, and see what your data can show you.

Visual 1 — Donut Chart: Sales by Category

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the Report View canvas.
  2. In the Visualizations pane (right side), click the Donut Chart icon (looks like a donut ring).
  3. From the Fields pane, drag category (from the sales table) into the Legend area.
  4. Drag amount (from the sales table) into the Values area.
  5. You should see a donut with slices for each food category.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Your donut should show category names (not category IDs). If you see codes instead of names, you may have dragged the wrong column. The category column came from your Menu Items merge — it is proof that merge worked.

Visual 2 — Table: Top Items by Sales

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas.
  2. In the Visualizations pane, click the Table icon (looks like a grid).
  3. Drag item_name into the table.
  4. Drag amount into the table.
  5. The table should show item names with their total sales, automatically sorted.
  6. To show only the top 10: click the table visual, then click the Filter icon on the visual header (or look in the Filters pane on the right). Under item_name, change the filter type to Top N, set it to 10, and drag amount into the "By value" field. Click Apply filter.
🛑 STOP AND CHECK Your table should show 10 rows with item names like "Cubano Sandwich" or "Mango Tango Smoothie" alongside their total sales. If the Top N filter feels tricky, skip it — a full table with all items still works. You can always come back to it.
💜 TAKE A BREATH You are building a real dashboard from data you merged yourself. This is what Power BI analysts do every day. Take a moment to look at what you have built — three visuals, each one pulling from a different merge.

Visual 3 — Bar Chart: Sales by Employee

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas.
  2. In the Visualizations pane, click the Clustered Bar Chart.
  3. Drag first_name into the Y-axis.
  4. Drag amount into the X-axis.
  5. You should see a bar for each employee showing their total sales.

Visual 4 — Slicer: Filter by Truck

✅ DO THIS
  1. Click an empty area of the canvas.
  2. In the Visualizations pane, click the Slicer icon (looks like a funnel with a list).
  3. Drag truck_name into the slicer.
  4. You should see a list of truck names. Click one of them.
  5. Watch what happens — all of your other visuals should update to show only the data for that truck.
💡 WHY ARE WE DOING THIS? When you clicked a truck name, every visual on the page filtered to show only that truck's data. This is called cross-filtering — Power BI automatically connects your visuals because they all share the same underlying data model. You did not write any code or set up any filters manually. This is a preview of the data modeling concepts you will learn in Week 12.

Visual 5 — Dashboard Title

✅ DO THIS
  1. Go to Insert tab (in the main Power BI ribbon at the top).
  2. Click Text box.
  3. A text box appears on the canvas. Type: Sabor Miami Sales Dashboard.
  4. Highlight the text. Use the formatting toolbar above the text box to make it bold and increase the font size to 20–24.
  5. Drag the text box to the top of your report page.

Arrange and Screenshot

Resize and arrange your visuals so everything fits on one page without overlapping. There is no single "right" layout — just make sure all 5 visuals and the title are visible at the same time.

✅ DO THIS
  1. Drag visuals by their edges to resize them. Drag from the center to reposition.
  2. Make sure no visuals overlap and the title is at the top.
  3. Click the slicer and select (Select all) so the dashboard shows all trucks.
  4. Take a screenshot: press Print Screen (or Windows + Shift + S to select a region).
  5. Click the paste zone below and press Ctrl + V.
